How we tested

This is a benchmark check, not a full validation. We ran the model for situations that match published Swiss studies and asked whether the measured values fall where the model puts them.

  • Data: published measurements from FOEN reports, the national soil, groundwater and air monitoring networks (NABO, NAQUA, NABEL), cantonal reports and peer-reviewed studies. Every number below is quoted from its source.
  • Model runs: the platform's own engine, with its default 400 Monte Carlo runs. "Model" gives the best estimate and, in brackets, the 90% range.
  • The main weakness: the studies rarely report every input the model needs (soil pH, organic carbon, area, release history) for the same site. We used the closest preset and say so. A true validation needs inputs and measurements from the same sites.
  • We did not tune the model to these data. Changing defaults to fit the same data used to test them would make the test meaningless. Where the model was off, we changed what the data clearly supports (labels, warnings, new inputs) and left the numbers alone otherwise.

What holds up: lead and arsenic concentrations in seepage and groundwater, how metals split between water and particles in rivers, where mercury sits in contaminated Valais soils, and slow change in lead-polluted topsoil.

What does not: the model's timing of arrival in groundwater (metals arrive in decades where slow matrix flow predicts thousands of years), antimony seepage in the field (the model uses the lab solubility), and the loss of mercury from topsoil (faster in reality than leaching and erosion explain).

Shooting ranges: lead and antimony

Seven Swiss 300 m ranges were studied for FOEN in 2001 (site names anonymised in the report). Model run: the platform's shooting-range example, which is the Swiss Plateau preset with metallic lead at 400 kg/yr for 50 years on 500 m², a 50 cm berm and a 10% reactive fraction. It gives 60,900 mg/kg lead in the berm, inside the measured 1,443–515,800 mg/kg.

1. Lead in eluate from bullet-trap soil

Matches
Measured
40–700 µg/L at 5 of 7 ranges; about 6 and 180 mg/L at the other two.
Lab eluate: soil < 0.5 mm shaken 24 h with distilled water at water:solid 10:1.
Model
Seepage water 120 µg/L (8.1–720)
Note
The model gives pore water, not a lab eluate. The two are almost equal when Kd is far above 10 L/kg, as it is for lead (52,000 L/kg here).

2. Lead in groundwater directly below the ranges

Matches
Measured
0.5–3 µg/L at 4 ranges with groundwater pH 6.4–7.5; about 30 µg/L at the acidic site
Model
Groundwater under the source, long term: 2.7 µg/L (0.44–22)

3. How fast metals reach groundwater

Does not match
Measured
Lead and antimony were already in the groundwater directly below ranges used for decades. The report names preferential flow paths as a key factor.
Model
Travel time to the water table by slow matrix flow: 1.2 million years (90,000 to 16 million)
Meaning
The concentration the model expects once the metal arrives is right (test 2), but the arrival time is wrong by orders of magnitude. Metals most likely take fast paths, such as cracks and root channels, or travel on fine particles.
Changed
The travel-time result no longer shows green "Beyond horizon". It now shows "Matrix flow only" as a result to check, with this evidence beside it. A new fast-path share input (Budget & floods tab) lets you test quick arrival.

5. Antimony in field seepage

Does not match
Measured

One lysimeter study with one soil is too little to set a new national default. The antimony results now show the lysimeter values beside the lab eluates, so users see that field seepage can be 50 times lower.

Rivers and lakes

Model runs: a release to the river with the Swiss Plateau preset and suspended sediment set to the measured 5.6 mg/L.

Dissolved and total samples were taken on different schedules, so the ratio is approximate.

8. Dissolved lead share in the Rhine

Matches
Measured
Same station: dissolved Pb below detection in 12 of 13 samples (maximum 0.13 µg/L), while total Pb had a median of 0.18 µg/L. Lead is mostly particle-bound.
Model
26% dissolved (5–72%)
Note
A qualitative match: the data show "mostly particle-bound" but cannot give a share.

9. Particles retained in large lakes

Partly
Measured
Lake Brienz receives about 300 kt of suspended sediment a year and exports about 10 kt, so about 97% stays. Lake Geneva's sediment budget gives about 98%.
Model
90% of river particles settle (fixed until now)
Changed
The trapping efficiency is now an input (Budget & floods tab), with the measured values in its help text. We kept 90% as the default because metals ride on the finest particles, which settle least, so metal retention is likely below bulk-sediment retention. A lake water residence time input was added for each preset (Lake Zurich 1.2, Lake Neuchâtel 8.2, Lake Geneva 11.4, Lake Lugano north basin 11.9 years).

Mercury in Upper Valais

Soils near Visp and Raron were polluted by mercury from a chemical factory, mainly through canal sediment spread on fields. Model: Visp preset, 2% organic carbon, Hg(II) bound to organic matter (Kd = Koc·foc, log Koc 5.5).

10. Water-soluble mercury in Valais soils

Matches
Measured
Soils with 0.5–28.4 mg/kg Hg and pH 6.2–8.1: water extraction at solid:liquid 1:100 released 0.1–1.5% of the total Hg.
Model
1.6% (0.1–18%) at 1:100, from Kd 6,300 L/kg
Note
The best estimate sits at the upper edge of the measured range, so the model leans towards more mobile mercury.

11. Mercury in soil solution

Partly
Measured
A floodplain soil with 2.38 mg/kg Hg and 3.45% organic carbon: 25–160 ng/L dissolved (< 0.02 µm) in lab microcosms.
Model
220 ng/L (15–3,100) at 3.45% organic carbon, if all the Hg can dissolve
Meaning
Inside the range, but the best estimate is 1.4–9 times too high: cautious again.

12. Mercury in Valais groundwater

Matches
Measured
No mercury above the 0.01 µg/L quantification limit in any groundwater campaign so far, including March 2024.
Model
For soil at 28 mg/kg: the metal has not reached the water table, and matrix flow needs about 29,000 years (2,200–390,000)
Note
Consistent, though test 3 shows the timing can be too slow where fast paths exist.

Soil trends and groundwater arsenic

The national soil monitoring network (NABO) has resampled about 100 sites every five years since 1985. If the model's long topsoil half-lives are right, topsoil concentrations should change only slowly.

13. Lead trend in Swiss topsoils

Partly
Measured
1985–2009: unchanged at 70 sites, lower at 29, higher at 1. The authors explain the decline by cleaner air and the ban on leaded petrol, and by tillage and soil animals mixing topsoil into the subsoil.
Model
Topsoil half-life about 1,500 years on arable land, 3,100 under garden or grass and 11,000 under forest, so less than 2% change in 24 years
Meaning
It explains why most sites did not change, but not the decline at a third of them, because mixing into deeper soil is not in the model.

14. Mercury trend in Swiss topsoils

Does not match
Measured
1985–2009: lower at 63 sites, unchanged at 27, higher at 3.
Model
Topsoil half-life about 670–1,100 years since version 0.11.0, which adds Hg⁰ emission from the soil (10 ng/m²/h per mg/kg). That is still only about 2–2.5% change in 24 years.
Meaning
Mercury leaves topsoil faster than the model explains. Hg⁰ emission at published flux rates adds only a small loss, so mixing into deeper soil is probably the main missing process.
Changed
Mercury soil results show the NABO trend beside them. Version 0.11.0 adds Hg⁰ emission from soil at published flux rates (Air & surface tab). Mixing into deeper soil can be tested with Other losses (Soil tab); we add no default for it because NABO reports no rate.

15. Arsenic in Plateau groundwater

Matches
Measured
NAQUA 2018: As above 10 µg/L at 2 of 524 stations and above 5 µg/L at 7. On the Plateau and in the Prealps, mostly below 1 µg/L.
Model
Seepage from Plateau soil with 8 mg/kg natural As: 0.70 µg/L (0.04–13), before any dilution in the aquifer

16. Arsenic in crystalline-rock groundwater

Not testable
Measured
Values above 5 µg/L occur almost only in crystalline fractured-rock aquifers in the Alps and south of the Alps; 10 µg/L is exceeded at one station each in Valais and Graubünden.
Model
Seepage from Ticino soil with 25 mg/kg natural As: 1.1 µg/L (0.05–20)
Meaning
The high values come from arsenic minerals in the rock, not from soil, so this is outside what the soil route models. Enter local groundwater arsenic as the background value. The result text now says this.

Air

17. Lead deposition from air

Partly: the shape
Measured
NABEL 2024 annual means: 1.2 µg/(m²·d) at rural Payerne up to 8.9 at Lausanne city centre, all far below the 100 µg/(m²·d) limit. Around the Gerlafingen steel works in 2010: 92 µg/(m²·d) next to the plant, falling to 4–15 nearby, against 3 at the reference site. The canton notes the dust travels only a few hundred metres.
Model
Since version 0.11.0: a plume averaged over wind sectors with dry and wet deposition by particle size. For a low, dusty source (10 m, 40% large dust), deposition falls about 8× from 100 to 500 m and 30× by 1 km. For fine particles from a 60 m stack it falls only about 6× over the same distance.
Meaning
The modelled fall-off for coarse dust is as steep as the Gerlafingen pattern, which supports the shape. The level still cannot be tested: no study gives both a plant's emission rate and the deposition around it. National lead emissions (about 15 t in 2018) spread over Switzerland would give at most 1 µg/(m²·d). Measured rural deposition is similar or higher, so resuspended dust and imports from abroad matter too.

What we changed

  • Travel time to the water table in gravel and sand: no longer green when it is beyond the horizon. It now reads "Matrix flow only" and shows the shooting-range evidence (test 3).
  • New fast-path share input, so the mass budget can send part of the seepage straight to groundwater (test 3).
  • Lake trapping efficiency and lake residence time are now inputs, with measured Swiss values in the help text (test 9).
  • Mercury: the NABO trend is shown beside soil results, and a suggestion to test Other losses is added (test 14).
  • Shooting-range benchmarks: labelled correctly as lab eluates (water:solid 10:1, < 0.5 mm), with field lysimeter values added for antimony (tests 1, 5, 6).
  • New "Measured" lines on results for river dissolved share, lake settling, Hg methylation, topsoil after the horizon, travel time and groundwater arsenic.

What we did not change, and why

  • Antimony defaults (tests 5 and 6): only one field lysimeter study exists, and fitting the defaults to the same data would hide the problem rather than fix it.
  • Kd relations: where results are off, they are high (cautious), and the measured values still fall inside the uncertainty range.

What this cannot tell you

  • Site-level accuracy. Inputs and measurements were never from the same site, so a match means "plausible for this kind of situation", not "right for your site".
  • The mass budget. Only the lake retention step was checked. The shares in soil, groundwater and lake have no Swiss dataset to test against yet.
  • Floods. The flood settings are assumptions; no Swiss event data were used.
  • Assumptions. Every default and its basis is listed on the assumptions page; those marked as developer assumptions need expert review.
  • The forms by phase. The form rules are indicative shares from general geochemistry. Apart from the methylmercury share in lake sediment, they have not been checked against Swiss speciation data.
  • The spread map. The plume equation, the terrain direction and the runoff path have not been checked against Swiss plume or tracer data.
  • Lake processes and the redox ladder. The Hg⁰ escape rate, the season factors and the trophic-state rules are assumptions scaled from studies outside Switzerland. The redox ladder is an electron balance without kinetics; it has not been checked against Swiss redox profiles.
  • Exports. The PHREEQC and MODFLOW 6 files were run to check they work, not to check their results against field data.
  • Air and land surface. Deposition velocities, washout, the size split by form, snow re-emission, leaf uptake, road partition and glacier ice concentrations come from literature outside these tests or are assumptions; only the fall-off with distance (test 17) was compared with Swiss data.
  • Air releases. See test 17.
  • Few sites. Several tests rest on one station or a handful of sites.

What would make it a real validation

  • Paired site data from cantonal contaminated-site registers or consultants' reports: soil, eluate, seepage and groundwater from the same place.
  • NADUF river data with dissolved and particulate metals measured in the same samples.
  • Field lysimeter or seepage data for lead, antimony and arsenic.
  • A topsoil mercury loss rate from NABO or flux studies.

Reproduce the runs

Every model number here can be rerun in the browser. Open the platform, open the developer console (F12), and call SMP.run(metal, inputs). Inputs you leave out take the preset values. The call returns all results with their 90% ranges, the mass budget, the uncertainty drivers and the internal rates.

// Tests 1–6: shooting-range example
SMP.run("Pb", {preset:"plateau", medium:"soil", form:"pbm", pattern:"continuous",
  amount:400, duration:50, area:500, depthMix:50, land:"agri", reactive:10})

// Tests 7–8: river release at 5.6 mg/L suspended sediment
SMP.run("As", {preset:"plateau", medium:"river", form:"asV", amount:1, ssc:5.6})

// Test 12: Valais soil at about 28 mg/kg Hg
SMP.run("Hg", {preset:"visp", medium:"soil", form:"hgsed", pattern:"pulse",
  amount:2.3, area:200, depthMix:30, land:"garden", reactive:50})

Tests 10 and 11 follow directly from the Kd relation: dissolved share = V/(Kd·M + V) for an extraction with V litres of water per M kg of soil, and pore water = Csoil/Kd.
